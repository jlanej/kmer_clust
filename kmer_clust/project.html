Projection prototype

Any assembly can be sketched with the same lottery, matched against the frozen vocabulary, and dropped onto this map — no aligner. Below: 4 Mb slices of the hard chr21 acrocentric region from two HPRC samples (plus a CHM13 self-slice as control), and a showcase fished out of the whole HG002 paternal assembly (29,337 windows projected, ~10 ms each): windows whose best exact locus falls in a famous region — MHC, MAPT, IGH, the 8p23.1 defensins, KIR, Yq12 — found by vocabulary alone; plus one whole chrY contig per sample (HG002, HG005) and a data-driven chr13 centromere entry surfaced by the coverage meter. Each set's one-liner appears under the picker. Each window travels four stations: its own assembly coordinates → its place in word-space → its loci on T2T (a solid stem to its best locus, thinner stems to runner-ups — one origin, all its homes) → fine placement, a second genome axis where every window lands at once at its exact-Jaccard-weighted position. Dots are painted by assembly order (1 → N, blue → orange): if fine placement lays the gradient out smoothly the projection is collinear with the assembly; a reversed run is an inversion, a shuffle is the commons — the τ readout on the fine axis scores it. Click a window to pin it — its world-line through all four stations draws at once and its loci light up in the views above; the paths toggle draws every world-line together, direct hides the two inner stations and straightens every path into an assembly ↔ placement ribbon (order and orientation at a glance), and zoom locus opens a per-locus detail view for the pinned window. Hover for hits and vocabulary coverage.
