CHM13 Word Atlas

A map of the complete human genome in which position is decided purely by k‑mer vocabulary — no aligner, no reference coordinate, no orthology. Every dot is one of bins of 100 kb — sketched by FracMinHash, embedded by randomized SVD → UMAP, grouped by HDBSCAN into vocabulary clusters. Bins land next to each other because they use the same words. Annotation judges the map; it never builds it.

Map ↔ territory

The map is the UMAP plane; the territory is the genome in its own coordinates. Hover either side and the other answers. Click a dot, a stripe, a legend chip, or a table row to isolate one cluster — legend chips and rows toggle, and shift-clicking dots adds their cluster, so several selections can glow at once. Pinch or double-click to zoom the map, drag to pan, and shift-drag (or the ⬚ select tool) to box a region — the territory and a region report answer. Sweep the k slider to change the vocabulary itself: every stop is a full re-sketch of the genome at that word length, layouts Procrustes-aligned so the morph shows structure, not spin. How much should you trust it?

Four stress tests. The two-way test is the important one: alpha-satellite HOR bins should be strongly identifiable by chromosome from vocabulary alone (arrays are chromosome-specific), while rDNA bins should be far weaker (the arrays recombine across the five acrocentrics). Both directions come out as biology predicts — αSat sits an order of magnitude above chance, rDNA barely doubles it — and the αSat confusions that do occur are the known shared HOR families. A method that secretly encodes position would ace both; one blind to fine vocabulary would fail the first.

Method

Words. Cut CHM13v2.0 (chr1–22, X, Y) into 100 kb bins and treat each bin as a document whose words are its 21-letter subsequences: every sliding window, with a word and its reverse complement counted as the same word (each is read in whichever direction is alphabetically first). A bin holds ~100,000 words; the genome holds ~2 billion distinct ones — far too many to compare directly, so we sample.

The lottery (FracMinHash). Every word goes through a hash function — a fixed scrambler that assigns it an effectively random number — and is kept only if that number falls below a bar set to pass 1 word in 20. The trick is that the lottery is rigged identically everywhere: a given word gets the same number on chr1 as on chr21, so it is kept in every bin that contains it or in none. That makes any two sketches directly comparable, and makes their overlap an unbiased estimate of the overlap of the full vocabularies. (This is sourmash's FracMinHash — MurmurHash3, seed 42, keep when hash ≤ 264/20 — and our engine is verified bit-identical to it.) Each kept word also carries its within-bin count, because satellite DNA expresses identity by saying a few words many times. And because a shared 21-letter word is essentially never a coincidence (421 ≈ 1,400× the genome), sharing vocabulary means sharing ancestry.

The matrix. Bins × kept-words — literally the word-count matrix of text retrieval, with the same weighting text search uses: words private to a single bin can connect nothing and are set aside (each bin's private fraction becomes the "private vocabulary" color mode); the rest are weighted by log-count, damped by how many bins use them (a rare shared word says more than a ubiquitous Alu core), and each bin is scaled to unit length so large and small vocabularies compare fairly. Two bins are similar when they spend their vocabulary in the same proportions — cosine similarity, exactly as in document search.

The map. The matrix still has ~2 million word-columns, so randomized SVD finds the 128 recurring word-combinations — think themes — that explain most of it, giving each bin 128 coordinates. UMAP then builds every bin's nearest-neighbor graph in that space and flattens it, preserving who-sits-next-to-whom rather than global distances: a seating chart, not a scale drawing. Position on this page therefore encodes one thing only: these bins use the same words.

The clusters. HDBSCAN finds crowds in a 12-dimensional version of the same layout — regions dense enough to count as a group — with no preset number of clusters and an explicit noise label for bins that belong to no crowd. Clusters are named afterwards from annotation, which never enters the model.

Distance, exactly (track B). The map is a model, so we also do it the slow way: boost bins to 1 Mb (sketches of ~47,000 words each), intersect every pair, and report exact Jaccard — the shared fraction of the union. One sparse matrix product yields all ~4.9 million pairs, and the model must agree with this ground truth or lose; that agreement is measured on the scorecard above, not assumed.

Projection. A segment from any assembly is placed in four moves. First it is sketched with the identical lottery — same words, same tickets — so each 100 kb window arrives as a bag of kept words directly comparable with every T2T bin, along with a coverage score: the fraction of its words CHM13 has ever seen anywhere (a per-window novelty meter). Second, where in word-space: the window's words that belong to the shared vocabulary take the frozen training weights and pass through the frozen SVD basis into the same 128 coordinates; its cosine-nearest bins are its dialect neighborhood, and it is drawn at their similarity-weighted center. Third, which locus: the window's full sketch — private words included — is intersected with every bin's full sketch in one sparse product, and exact Jaccard ranks the candidates. This second signal exists because euchromatic street addresses live precisely in the private words the model excludes: cosine alone names the right locus 30% of the time; exact Jaccard, 97%. The top hits are then chained into loci — genomically adjacent bins merge into one, distinct places stay distinct — so a window that belongs to several regions reports up to three, each carrying its own J; within the best locus, the J-weighted centroid of the member bins gives a landing point at 100 kb resolution. Validated by self-projection of half-overlapping T2T windows: top-1 97% (euchromatin) / 70% (satellite & acrocentric, where within-array ties are the honest residue), top-3 99% / 92%. One transparency note: the SVD contributes compression, not information, to projection — full-rank shared-vocabulary cosine lands windows in the same place (median displacement 0.01% of the map span) — and loci never touch it at all; it is used because the map itself is that basis flattened, so queries are placed in the display's own geometry. The showcase sets in the projection panel run this machinery over an entire HPRC haplotype (HG002 paternal, 29,337 windows, ~10 ms each) and keep the windows whose best exact locus falls in a famous region; their exact-J levels form a divergence dial — Yq12 ≈0.88 (CHM13's chrY is HG002's), IGH 0.87, SMN and 22q11.2 0.79, MHC 0.62, 8p23.1 defensins 0.56, MAPT/17q21.31 0.35 (the H1/H2 inversion: most word-divergent, still placed exactly) — and the KIR window of the leukocyte receptor complex carries 20% vocabulary CHM13 has never seen, the coverage meter reading a haplotype that differs in gene content.

Order & novelty. Projected windows are painted by their order along the assembly segment (blue → orange), and Kendall's τ between that order and each window's fine placement — placements within 10 kb count as tied — is printed on the fine axis: |τ| ≥ 0.85 collinear (inverted when negative), ≥ 0.5 mostly ordered, else scrambled. The direct view straightens every path into an assembly↔placement ribbon, so order and orientation read at a glance. Coverage — the fraction of a window's words the reference has ever seen — is an unbiased novelty meter, because the sketch lottery is a deterministic function of the word: an absent word is genuinely absent from the reference. Sets that trim or thin their windows say so directly on the assembly axis.

Views. The k slider re-sketches the genome at each word length 15–25. Long words mean identity — a shared 21-mer (421 ≈ 1,400× the genome) is evidence of common descent — while short words drift toward composition (415 is about the genome's own size). Every layout is Procrustes-aligned to k=21, so morphing shows structure, not spin. The paired views concatenate two per-k models, each L2-normalized so the pair's cosine is exactly the mean of the two vocabularies' cosines. That mean has two regimes: adjacent horizons mostly agree, and their consensus sharpens clusters (consensus k17⊕k21: ~2% HDBSCAN noise); complementary horizons disagree productively, maximizing information at the cost of flat-cluster confidence (info k15⊕k21: the best-organized euchromatin). Concatenating all six k's does worse than either — redundant middle horizons average the ends away. Cluster colors always come from the k=21 model.

Diagnostics. Tandem periodicity is the dominant spacing between repeats of the same kept hash within a bin — live αSat comes out at 7.99× the 171 bp monomer and rDNA at ~45 kb, with no annotation input. Acrocentric mixing is the fraction of a p-arm bin's nearest vocabulary neighbors (own-array adjacency excluded) that come from a different acrocentric: the pan-acrocentric commons made visible bin by bin.

Judged by the T2T censat v2.1, segdup, and RepeatMasker tracks, which color and score the finished map but contribute nothing to it.
