CHM13 Word Atlas

A map of the complete human genome in which position is decided purely by k‑mer vocabulary — no aligner, no reference coordinate, no orthology. Every dot is a 100 kb bin, sketched by FracMinHash, embedded by randomized SVD → UMAP, grouped by HDBSCAN. Bins land next to each other because they use the same words. Annotation judges the map; it never builds it.

Map ↔ territory

The map is the UMAP plane; the territory is the genome in its own coordinates. Hover either side and the other answers. Click a dot, a stripe, a legend chip, or a table row to isolate one cluster. Pinch or double-click to zoom the map, drag to pan, and shift-drag (or the ⬚ select tool) to box a region — the territory and a region report answer. Sweep the k slider to change the vocabulary itself: every stop is a full re-sketch of the genome at that word length, layouts Procrustes-aligned so the morph shows structure, not spin. Press esc to release a selection.

Distance, exactly

The map above is a model. This is not: every pair of 1 Mb bins compared by exact sketch intersection. The matrix shows exact Jaccard (∛-stretched so faint structure survives); the dendrogram ordering and pairwise clusters use max-containment ANI, cANI = C1/k, which is robust to the wild sketch-size differences between satellite and unique sequence. In genome order the diagonal blocks are chromosomes; flip to dendrogram order and the satellite families reassemble from across the genome. Hover for the pair behind any pixel.

hover the matrix…

Off-diagonal constellations in genome order are the biology: acrocentric short arms quoting each other, segmental duplications, satellite families shared between chromosomes.

How much should you trust it?

Four stress tests. The two-way test is the important one: alpha-satellite HOR bins should be strongly identifiable by chromosome from vocabulary alone (arrays are chromosome-specific), while rDNA bins should be far weaker (the arrays recombine across the five acrocentrics). Both directions come out as biology predicts — αSat sits an order of magnitude above chance, rDNA barely doubles it — and the αSat confusions that do occur are the known shared HOR families. A method that secretly encodes position would ace both; one blind to fine vocabulary would fail the first.

The clusters

Method

Sketch. Every 100 kb bin of CHM13v2.0 (chr1–22, X, Y) is reduced to a FracMinHash sketch: canonical 21-mers hashed with MurmurHash3-x64-128 (seed 42), kept when hash ≤ 264/scaled — bit-identical to sourmash, verified by test. The base store keeps scaled=20 with within-bin multiplicities; every coarser sketch is a subset of it.

Model (track A). Bins × shared-hashes matrix at scaled=50; hashes private to one bin are dropped from the model (each bin's private fraction is kept as a diagnostic — color the map by it). Weights log1p(multiplicity) × mild IDF, L2-normalized; randomized subspace SVD on the implicit Gram operator; UMAP (cosine) for display; HDBSCAN in a 12-d UMAP space for clusters.

Distance (track B). Bins boosted to 1 Mb so sketches are deep enough that pairwise Jaccard and containment are essentially exact, then one sparse matmul gives all ~4.9 M pairs. The two tracks agree or the model is wrong — that agreement is measured, not assumed.

Views. The k slider re-sketches the genome at each word length 15–25. Long words mean identity — a shared 21-mer (421 ≈ 1,400× the genome) is evidence of common descent — while short words drift toward composition (415 is about the genome's own size). Every layout is Procrustes-aligned to k=21, so morphing shows structure, not spin. The paired views concatenate two per-k models, each L2-normalized so the pair's cosine is exactly the mean of the two vocabularies' cosines. That mean has two regimes: adjacent horizons mostly agree, and their consensus sharpens clusters (consensus k17⊕k21: ~2% HDBSCAN noise); complementary horizons disagree productively, maximizing information at the cost of flat-cluster confidence (info k15⊕k21: the best-organized euchromatin). Concatenating all six k's does worse than either — redundant middle horizons average the ends away. Cluster colors always come from the k=21 model.

Diagnostics. Tandem periodicity is the dominant spacing between repeats of the same kept hash within a bin — live αSat comes out at 7.99× the 171 bp monomer and rDNA at ~45 kb, with no annotation input. Acrocentric mixing is the fraction of a p-arm bin's nearest vocabulary neighbors (own-array adjacency excluded) that come from a different acrocentric: the pan-acrocentric commons made visible bin by bin.

Judged by the T2T censat v2.1, segdup, and RepeatMasker tracks, which color and score the finished map but contribute nothing to it.
